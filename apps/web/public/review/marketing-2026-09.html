02The position

ETI360 is risk intelligence for school trips. We turn the information a school and its providers already hold — itineraries, provider documents, dates, routes, hazards, and controls — into consistent, decision-ready records for planning, the days away, and post-trip review. We prepare the structured working documents and provide the application that keeps the itinerary, evidence, review status, comments, and versions organized. The school or provider reviews, completes, and approves its own risk assessment and RAMS. ETI360 does not approve or certify trips, and the judgment remains with the school.

The reference register is International SOS, Crisis24, The Economist: a premium intelligence service. Never EdTech, never generic SaaS, never government-compliance gray, never adventure-travel lifestyle. The three customer-facing tiers are Tier 1 Organizational Baseline (the school's standing governance position, refreshed annually), Tier 2 Trip Risk Review (the consistent trip file leadership sees before approving a trip), and Tier 3 Dynamic Risk Operations (the school-operated Duty Manager Dashboard and the record a trip carries home).

The ask is the same everywhere: a 20-minute walkthrough of the worked example, compared with how the school currently runs things. No free deliverables (Dan, September 3): the showcase carries the proof — real, openable documents from the fictitious-by-design reference school — so a school can judge the work before any engagement.

03The formula

Why the campaign leads with problems, and the rules that keep it honest.

Services like ours default to describing what they make: frameworks, tiers, document libraries. Buyers do not shop for documents. They carry questions — practical, operational questions that come up in every planning cycle and every week a trip is traveling. An earlier draft of this campaign worked backward from what the product renders to questions those outputs could answer; the August review named that mistake precisely — the right presentation instinct, the wrong discovery method — and reversed the direction. Start with the difficulties school leaders already face, and use the artifact as evidence that the difficulty has been addressed, not as the thing being sold.

The governing formula: one question. One problem. One ETI360 answer. One visible proof.
The question itself carries the problem — a school leader should be able to read it, answer yes or no, and immediately understand why the capability matters. Copy moves directly from question to answer, with no paragraph on the consequences of not having the service.

The whole program is one argument made at three depths. The email carries one problem and its answer. The questions hub carries the set. The detail page carries the proof. Every layer hands the reader to the next in the same voice with the same offer, which shrinks the cold email's job to something a cold email can actually do: earn one click on one recognizable problem.

Two disciplines hold it together. First, every claim stays inside our boundaries (section 05). Second, we sell the solution, never fear of its absence. No incident stories, no urgency, no implied negligence. The purpose of each question is recognition, not alarm; the emotional destination of every page and every email is clarity, confidence, and preparedness. A reader's honest "no" to one of our questions should end in "I want that," not in worry. Avoiding fear does not mean avoiding the problem — workload, ambiguity, inconsistency, and stale information can all be named factually, without blaming the school or selling catastrophe.

04The questions

The public face of the direction: questions a school leader can answer in the time it takes to read them, each answered by a real output.

The set follows the life of a trip — prepare, run, improve — and it is deliberately open. These are where we start, not a catalog of everything a trip can raise: when a school or trip provider brings a question that is not on the page, we work on it with them, and the answer joins the library. Never present the set with a count ("nine questions" is banned copy); it reads as a partnership, not a product boundary.

05Voice and claims

The rules every piece follows. Full document: docs/ETI360_TONE_AND_VOICE.md in the working repo. Breaking these is the fastest way to undo a month of careful positioning.

Register

  • Economist-influenced: evidence-based, restrained, precise.
  • One English version only. All copy uses American English, regardless of the recipient's country. Regional legal requirements may affect whether and how a message is sent, but they do not create separate English-language copy versions.
  • Moment-first, benefit-led: open on the reader's moment (the sign-off meeting, the days away, the phone held overnight) — never on what a school lacks, never fear or liability framing, never document counts.
  • Output-first: show what the school receives — the page, the screen, the measured figure — before explaining how the system works. Real artifacts over descriptions.
  • Specificity over adjectives: exact numbers when available (80.0 km, 183 m, 4h 45), never superlatives, never urgency, never unverifiable comparative claims.
  • Banned register: emojis, exclamation marks, SaaS vocabulary (seamless, powerful, cutting-edge), EdTech vocabulary (empowers, transforms, unlocks potential).

Claims boundaries

  • ETI360 structures, checks, evaluates, organizes, presents, and supports review. It never approves, certifies, ensures safety, or manages risk on the school's behalf. The decision stays with the school.
  • ETI360 prepares and organizes the structured working documents, itinerary information, provider documentation, hazards, controls, and supporting evidence used to complete the risk assessment and RAMS. The school or provider reviews, completes, amends as necessary, and approves the final documents. ETI360 does not approve or certify them. SOPs are supplied by the provider or school for evaluation.
  • The ETI360 application keeps the evidence, review status, comments, and versions organized. It supports the workflow but does not transfer decision-making or approval responsibility from the school or provider.
  • The school or provider identifies the medical facilities in the trip plan. ETI360 verifies the available information, maps the facilities, and connects them to relevant itinerary locations. ETI360 does not recommend a hospital or decide which facility should be used during an emergency.
  • The Duty Manager Dashboard and incident record are school-operated. ETI360 is not a monitoring service. Scheduled group location, never live tracking of students. Do not soften either line.
  • Historical climate information describes conditions recorded for the relevant locations and time of year. It must always be labeled as historical and must never be presented as a forecast.
  • Harborview International School is disclosed as fictitious every time a Harborview artifact is shown.

Naming canon

  • Tiers: Tier 1 Organizational Baseline · Tier 2 Trip Risk Review · Tier 3 Dynamic Risk Operations. "Assurance" is retired.
  • Duty Manager Dashboard — never "Live" prefixes, never "Ops Center" names.
  • "Trips" or "current trips," never "fleet." "Incident type," never "event family."
  • Baseline surfaces show At standard / Progressing states (blue), never percentages. The capability framework presents as the ETI360 Operational Capability Framework — ten areas; standards alignment stays internal. Its id codes (OC01–OC10) appear only inside document renders, beside the evidence they index — never recited in marketing or narrative copy (Dan, September 2: if the sentence reads the same without the code, the code was noise).
  • ARP (Activity Risk Profile) is fully retired from customer surfaces — RAMS carries the risk story alone. Legacy ARP renders still sit in the asset folders; never reuse them on anything customer-facing.
  • "Risk intelligence for school trips" is the approved brand line and must remain visible across core website and campaign surfaces. Other taglines, including "Every trip, decision-ready," are retired. A website hero may use a plain service descriptor, but the approved brand line must remain within the page hierarchy, navigation, or footer. "Start with one trip" remains retired as a slogan; the one-trip offer is phrased plainly.
  • Signature everywhere: Dan Skimin, Co-Founder & Principal Consultant.
Every piece of customer-facing copy passes the full tone review (the eti360-tone-review gate) before final render, every time the copy changes. This is mandatory, not advisory. The gate runs in the ETI360 working repo on Dan's machine — request it from Dan whenever copy changes; the full voice rules live there too (docs/ETI360_TONE_AND_VOICE.md).

How sending works

  • Personal Gmail, no mail platform. Drafts are prepared per school from Dan's own account (danskimin@eti360.com), reviewed individually, and sent personally. Merge tags do not exist; the [Name] placeholder and first line are completed per school. A plain reply-to-decline line replaces any unsubscribe machinery: "If this is not for you, reply 'no thanks' and I will leave it there." Opt-outs are processed the same day.
  • A spreadsheet is the CRM (to be created before wave 1): one row per school — featured solution, contact, status, sent date, replied, opted out, briefing requested. Follow-ups gate on the sheet, never the calendar: any reply or "no thanks" suppresses the school from all further touches the same day.
  • Follow-up touches: not yet designed for v17. The old two-touch spine belonged to the retired arms and the retired offer. Decide before wave 1: send wave 1 with no follow-up, or write and gate a single v17 follow-up (a plain second note restating the conversation ask).
  • Deliverability guardrails: SPF and DKIM verified before batch one; seed tests across Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail including an images-off read; a one-day bulk verification pass over the list before sending (hard bounces and dead domains dropped). Hard rule: any bounce or complaint spike pauses the next batch until it is understood. Every send goes out from the account the business runs on — a complaint spike can suspend it, which is why the pace stays small and personal.
  • Contact sourcing rules (approved, standing): public sources only — school websites, accreditation announcements, press releases, public LinkedIn viewed by hand. Emails published or role-based (admissions@, office@, info@ for the attention of the named person), never pattern-guessed. No purchased lists, no email-finder services, no verification-by-sending. Every name, title, and address records its source URL and capture date, so "where did you get this?" gets a straight answer.
  • Who we write to: school leadership — Head of School, Deputy or Secondary Principal, Director of Operations, Business Manager, or the Trip/Activities Coordinator. Where only an admissions or general inbox is published, the email goes to that inbox for the attention of the named leader.
  • Who presses send: everything goes out from Dan's own accounts — email from his mailbox, posts from his profile. You draft and review; Dan sends and publishes.
