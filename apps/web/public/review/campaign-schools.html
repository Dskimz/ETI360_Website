Campaign rollout · the school list

Who the campaign goes to, and in what order.

Companion to the campaign review. One hundred and fifty schools across three weeks, fifty a week, drawn from our directory of 510 international schools in 106 countries.

The ordering

Latin America first, then the world. Week one takes every Latin America and Caribbean school in the directory, forty-four of them, topped up with the six strongest North American entries. From week two the campaign moves outward: North America, then Europe. Within each week, schools with the strongest published evidence of an active trip program go first.

After these three weeks roughly 350 schools remain in the directory, enough for seven further waves at the same pace.

The recipient column is empty because the data does not exist. The directory carries school names, cities and websites; it holds no contact people and no email addresses at all. The domain column is the starting point for that research. Recipient names and addresses are gathered per school at drafting time and are deliberately never published on this page or stored on the website.

Corrections applied to this list

Two were requested. Checking them turned up a third, and changed the shape of the first.

Location corrected

  • Colegio Jorge Washington was filed as Washington D.C., United States. Its own domain is cojowa.edu.co and the school is in Cartagena, Colombia. Corrected, and moved from week two into week one, which is why the weeks now run 51, 49 and 50.

Five records repaired rather than deleted

The records flagged as corrupted are not junk. In each one a comma inside the school name pushed the rest of the name into the website field, so the school is real and the entry is simply split. Deleting them would have dropped five genuine prospects, one of them in week one. The names are restored below and their websites are researched with the recipient.

Two of those five also carried the wrong country for the same reason, which is corrected above. One more to watch: the directory holds both "St George's International School Luxembourg" and "St. George's International School" in Switzerland, which may or may not be a duplicate; they are treated as separate schools until someone checks.

Left as they are

  • A handful of week two and three entries are elite boarding schools rather than international schools. They are still plausible buyers and stay in the list.
