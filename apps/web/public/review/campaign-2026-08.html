Section 1

The campaign in brief.

Introduce ETI360 to international schools, show them finished work, and open conversations that lead to paid engagements.

This is the first outbound campaign. It runs on two channels at once, both organic, with no advertising spend. There is no mailing platform: every email is written for one school and sent personally.

ElementHow it works
Who it goes toInternational schools that run overseas and residential trips, from our own directory of 510 schools in 106 countries. Fifty a week for three weeks, Latin America first, then outward. The full rollout list.
Who we are speaking toThe person who owns the trip-approval process: the head, a deputy, or a director of operations. Trip coordinators are the people the work equips, and they influence whether it is adopted.
EmailThree versions of the introduction, tested against each other. Written per school as personal Gmail messages, reviewed and sent by Dan. No merge tags, a plain reply-to-opt-out line, the Singapore registered address.
SocialOne story in eight posts over four weeks, two a week, from Dan's profile with the company page as the archive. The same posts run on Facebook in a shorter cut.
What we ask forA briefing: a short conversation about one upcoming trip. Nothing is produced without charge, and no prices appear in the emails.
What we showReal finished pages from Harborview International School, our reference school. It is fictitious by design, which is what lets us publish complete documents rather than redacted ones. That is disclosed everywhere its work appears.
How we measureEvery link is tagged so a visit can be traced to the arm and the school that produced it. Replies, briefings held, and engagements are counted by hand.

What happens after a reply

A reply leads to a twenty-minute briefing about one upcoming trip: the decision it is heading for, who will read its file, and what that file would need to contain. If the school wants to proceed, the work is quoted from the menu in section 3. The Duty Manager Simulation comes later, once a school's trips are in our system, because it runs on their real trip data.

Section 2

Position and voice.

1 · Core positioning line

ETI360 is an independent trip-governance service for international schools and educational travel providers.

2 · Full positioning statement

ETI360 provides independent trip-governance services for international schools and educational travel providers. The same documented framework is applied at three levels: to the organization each year, to each trip before departure, and while the trip runs. This structures the evidence behind key decisions and produces complete documents that schools can read, use, and retain.

ETI360 helps schools establish organizational readiness, review each trip on its own merits, and equip their teams for dynamic operations. It creates an independent, connected record that complements provider documentation, trip-approval software, and travel-assistance services. Decision-making authority remains with the school.

3 · Tagline

Educational Travel Insights.

The rules every piece of copy follows

  • Moment first, benefit led. Open on the reader's moment, never on what a school lacks. No fear framing, no liability framing, no urgency, no document counts.
  • Output first. Show what the school receives before explaining how the system works. Real pages, never mockups.
  • The boundary, stated plainly. ETI360 structures, identifies, surfaces and organizes. It never advises, certifies, approves or guarantees. The decision and the duty of care stay with the school.
  • Specific over adjectival. Exact figures where they exist. No superlatives, no unverifiable comparisons.
  • Naming. The tier names above, the Duty Manager Dashboard, trips rather than fleet. No external standard is named on a page a prospect can see; the framework presents as the ETI360 Operational Capability Framework.
  • American English. No em-dashes, no emojis, no exclamation marks.

One claim to confirm as you read: the emails state a combined thirty-plus years and more than ten thousand students. The years are right for the two of you. The student figure should have something behind it before it goes to fifty schools, so it is approval item 08.

Section 3

What we sell, and for how much.

The first trip is not the win. A school with fifty trips is.

Work is priced per document rather than sold as one package, so a school can start small. Revenue comes from the program, not the project: land inexpensively, review one trip properly, then take up the calendar. The account renews through the annual baseline and the following year's trips.

The ladder

  1. Door opener. The Organizational Baseline at an introductory price, for a school we are not yet working with.
  2. First trip. One trip review at the working price. The demonstration is paid for.
  3. The program. The year's calendar at per-trip pricing, with Dashboard access included while trips run.
  4. The Simulation. Once the school's trips are in the system, because it runs on their real trip data.
  5. Renewal. The baseline refresh and the next calendar.

What the Duty Manager Simulation is

It runs after a school's trips are in the system: itineraries built, procedures researched, risk assessments added, family documents produced. The session then uses their real trip data. Their duty manager works a timeline of those trips on the Duty Manager Dashboard, handling events as they arrive, to test their own procedures and their team's ability to run them. It is the second sale to an existing client, never the first sale to a stranger, which is why it does not appear in these emails.

No price appears on any public page or in any email. Prices live in proposals and conversations.

The real constraint on the first send

The directory holds school names, cities and websites. It holds no contact people and no email addresses at all, so every named recipient has to be researched before a draft can be written. The rollout list now carries each school's domain as the starting point and an empty recipient column to fill. This is the longest task standing between here and Monday, and it is not a writing task. If fifty are not ready, a first send to the twenty schools we know best beats slipping the week.

Section 7

How the pieces run.

The website

www.eti360.com is built with Next.js and hosted on Vercel. A push to the main branch rebuilds and publishes in about two minutes. Everything the emails and posts point to lives here.

Email

Campaign email goes out as personal Gmail messages, drafted per school and reviewed before sending. There is no mailing platform by choice. The website contact form delivers through Resend to Dan, and now also sends the enquirer an automatic acknowledgment signed by Dan.

Analytics

Vercel Web Analytics is live and cookieless, so the site sets no cookies and needs no consent banner. Campaign link tags land here. Google Analytics is wired but switched off; turning it on would change that cookie position.

The working app

The school portal, the Duty Manager Dashboard and the document packs run as a separate application on Render. Planned next step: move it to app.eti360.com so client links carry our own domain.

What is new on the website

  • The framework runs as a scrolling sequence on the homepage, one tier at a time, with real documents alongside.
  • A page explaining the Duty Manager Simulation.
  • One action across the site: Arrange a briefing.
  • No external standard is named anywhere a prospect can see.
  • The current eight-page baseline replaces an older one-page version.

One thing that must happen before Monday, and it is not copy

Every link in these emails points at www.eti360.com. The rewritten site is finished but still sits on a working branch. If it is not merged and published before the first send, recipients land on the previous version of the site, which carries the old positioning and would contradict the email that brought them there.
