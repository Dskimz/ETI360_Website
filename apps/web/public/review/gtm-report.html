ETI360 · Go-to-market strategy on one page · August 2026

ETI360 has built more credible proof than almost any pre-revenue firm of its size, and has attached none of it to a working demand system. The task is not to make better material. It is to put a measurable commercial mechanism behind the material that already exists.

The central marketing problem

Proof without a funnel. Fifteen openable PDFs, a working operations dashboard, a fully worked reference school, and a shipped website exist. There is no offer page, no source capture on the contact form, no conversion event firing, no recorded LinkedIn handle, and no price anywhere a prospect can see.

The market opportunity

International schools and their trip providers both answer the same governance questions, from different sides of the same trip. Nobody in the corpus-evidenced landscape owns the answer layer between approval-workflow software and enterprise duty-of-care services. That gap is narrow, defensible, and under-occupied.

Recommended positioning

The independent trip-governance service for international schools and the providers who serve them. Category: independent educational-travel review. Not a certifier, not a platform, not a tour operator.

Selected campaign platform

The Questions a Trip Must Answer. Every asset answers one real question a board, an insurer, a parent, or a duty manager asks, and names the page that carries the answer. The concept already exists on the site as a section heading. Elevate it to the platform.

Priority audience

Primary: the trip coordinator or activities lead at an international school with a documented trip program. Secondary: the operations or safety director at an international-school trip provider, who is both a buyer and a distribution channel into schools.

Role of the website

The proof surface and the only measured conversion point. It currently proves and does not convert: the homepage has no off-page call to action above the fold, and the offer every email points to has no page of its own.

Role of LinkedIn

Founder profile is the reach engine; the company page is the archive and the credibility check. Founder posts carry the questions; the page carries the artifacts and the record. Company page reach for a new page is near zero, so the page is not a distribution plan.

Role of Facebook

Minimal-maintenance verification surface. Publish the four cuts already written, keep details current, spend nothing further, and re-allocate the effort to reply handling and founder LinkedIn.

Principal conversion mechanism

A capped, instrumented one-trip review offer on its own page, with a scoped intake form that records source, followed by a paid Duty Manager Simulation as the first commercial engagement. Marketing today points at free work and never names the product that is for sale.

The single decision for leadership

Whose calendar wins the next ninety days of selling time: schools, or providers. The corpus contains two plans of record that answer this differently and both are live. Nothing downstream can be sequenced until one is named.

The first five actions

  1. Fix the launch-blocking copy defects before the first email sends. Eleven live surfaces name ISO 31031 against the naming law stamped the same day the site shipped; the retired one-trip slogan survives in the arm C email; the flagship Tier 1 PDF served by the site is a one-page document titled "ISO 31031 Assessment Report"; a public sample PDF says "Duty Manager Live Ops Center" and "Running the fleet."
  2. Build one offer page and instrument the intake. /one-trip, with hidden fields for UTM source, landing page, and referrer, and a conversion event that fires on submit. Every email and post then has a destination that can be measured.
  3. Resolve the UTM scheme to one convention and add utm_medium. Three different schemes are documented in the corpus and the live campaign links omit utm_medium entirely, which is the parameter analytics uses for channel grouping.
  4. Record the LinkedIn handle and publish from the founder profile first. The company page is an unnamed asset; until the handle exists in the repository, no link, no footer entry, and no measurement can point at it.
  5. Reorder wave 1 to Asia-Pacific. Of the 44 Latin America and Caribbean schools in wave 1, seven carry a documented trip program in ETI360's own directory. Asia carries 61. The campaign's own selection rule and its own ordering rule contradict each other.

Section 1

Executive recommendation.

The central marketing problem

Observed ETI360's problem is not awareness, not brand, and not proof quality. The corpus contains fifteen complete PDFs openable from the live site, ten more in the document library, a working Duty Manager Dashboard, four method diagrams, a full reference-school document set, three finished email arms with every link verified, an eight-post social story, a conference booth loop, and a 510-row school directory. Very few pre-revenue firms have this much finished, showable work.

Observed What does not exist is the mechanism that turns any of it into a booked conversation. The homepage's primary and secondary calls to action are both same-page anchors (#framework and #work), so the top of the site has no path out of itself. The contact form collects name, organization, role, email, country and message, and records nothing about where the visitor came from, so a UTM-tagged campaign can prove a page view and cannot prove an inquiry. Five conversion events are specified in apps/web/docs/ANALYTICS.md and, in that file's own words, nothing emits them yet. Google Analytics is wired and dormant. The LinkedIn company page exists and its handle is recorded in no repository. The conference booth loop contains no URL, no QR code, and no campaign parameter of any kind.

Inferred The result is a business that can show a prospect exactly what it produces and cannot tell which of its efforts produced a prospect. For a solo founder with a hard December revenue gate, that is the expensive failure: not a wasted campaign, but an unreadable one.

The sharpest single finding

Observed The Duty Manager Simulation is the sold product. The naming law and the business plan amendment of 2026-07-31 both state it: a facilitated ninety-minute session, pilot-priced at $3,000, with the written stress-test report surviving only as its After-Action Report. The word "Simulation" appears zero times in the campaign review page, zero times across all three email arms, zero times in the eight-post social story, zero times on the homepage, and zero times on the framework page. It appears as one row in a fifteen-row document library. The campaign about to be sent to 150 schools markets free work and never names the thing that is for sale.

The market opportunity

Observed ETI360's own competitive work, held in content/vault/Marketing/Research/, places three named categories around it: real-time incident detection (samdesk), enterprise duty of care with medical and security assistance (International SOS, Crisis24), and school approval-workflow software (EVOLVE and eduFOCUS in the UK, Xcursion in Australia). Its synthesis names the gap in its own words: K-12 governance-first design, transparent responsibility boundaries, and anti-false-precision controls.

Inferred That is a real and narrow position: the layer that turns a trip into reviewable evidence, sitting above template paperwork and below enterprise response services. It is defensible for exactly the reason it is small. Enterprise providers cannot serve it at their price point, and workflow platforms record the approval without producing the evidence the approval rests on. The opportunity is not a large market; it is an uncontested one at consultancy scale, which is the scale ETI360's own business plan sizes it at.

The recommended positioning

Recommended ETI360 is the independent trip-governance service for international schools and the providers who serve them. The category to occupy is independent educational-travel review: the outside party that structures the evidence a trip decision rests on, without ever taking the decision. Section 5 develops three options and explains why this one wins on defensibility, on search language, and on the only proof ETI360 actually has.

The selected campaign concept

Recommended The Questions a Trip Must Answer. Every campaign asset states one question a school is genuinely asked, states what a complete answer needs, and names the document that carries it. This structure already exists inside /for-schools and /for-providers as the QA blocks, and it is the strongest asset on the site. It is currently buried three quarters of the way down two pages and used nowhere else. Section 8 sets it against two credible alternatives and explains the selection.

The priority audience

Recommended Primary: the trip coordinator, activities lead, or head of outdoor education at an international school that already runs a documented trip program. They feel the work, they hold the itinerary, and they can start an engagement without a procurement process. Secondary: the operations or safety director at a provider selling into international schools, who buys faster, has a revenue motive, and puts ETI360's evidence in front of every school on their book. Section 4 sets out why the head of school is the approver and not the entry point.

Supporting headlines. "This is the whole document." · "Nothing here is a mockup." · "Read it the way a board would."

Website execution. Already built. The document rows and the marquee are this concept.

Primary audience. The founder's existing network in educational travel, and the second-degree connections it reaches.

Purpose. Reach and trust. This is the distribution channel.

Content role. The question and the judgment behind it. What the company page cannot say because a company cannot have an opinion.

The gap that matters most

Observed There is no defined step between "a school replies with an itinerary" and "a school pays for something." The campaign specifies what is sent, what is published, and what is offered, and stops at the reply. No acknowledgment template, no qualification criteria, no scope boundary on the free work, no defined moment at which the Simulation is proposed, and no proposal template.

Recommended Write four short documents before wave 1 sends. They are half a day of work and they are the difference between a campaign and a pipeline.

  1. The acknowledgment. Sent within one business day. Confirms receipt, names the documents that will come back, names the date, and asks one qualifying question: how many overseas or residential trips the school runs in a year.
  2. The scope note. One paragraph stating what the free review covers and what it does not, so the obligation has an edge.
  3. The handover call agenda. Fifteen minutes walking the delivered file, ten minutes on what would change if the school did this for every trip, five minutes proposing the Simulation.
  4. The proposal template. One page: the Simulation, its scope, its price, its date. The business plan already notes that no contract or proposal templates exist and that sales administration has no process.

Lead magnets, recommended and rejected

Recommended

  • The one-trip review. Already the offer. Capped, scoped, instrumented.
  • The Trip Approval Evidence Checklist. Useful to a school whether or not it buys, and useless to anyone else.
  • The pattern report. From month three. The strongest qualifier of the three because it interests only people who run trip programs.
  • A fifteen-minute framework walkthrough. Offered to anyone who downloads the checklist. Cheap, personal, and it converts a download into a conversation, which is where this business actually closes.

Rejected

  • A newsletter. Requires a list, an ESP, and a weekly obligation. All three contradict the standing email model and none of them is the constraint.
  • A destination guide. Would attract travelers and parents at volume. Wrong audience, and it would make the inbox less useful.
  • Any scoring tool, quiz or readiness calculator. Produces a number about a school's readiness, which is precisely what the Tier 1 rules forbid on customer surfaces, and it implies an assessment ETI360 does not make.
  • A webinar. High production cost, low attendance for a firm with no audience yet, and it competes with the fifteen-minute call for the same intent at ten times the effort.

The single decision ETI360 leadership must make

Whose calendar owns the next ninety days of selling time: schools, or providers?

The corpus contains two plans of record and they answer this differently. The business plan, the product packages document and the positioning reference all state provider-first: the provider is the buyer, the school is the audience the evidence must convince. The campaign now awaiting approval targets 150 schools and no providers. Neither document supersedes the other, both are live, and every downstream question depends on which one governs: which list gets worked, which page gets the traffic, which argument gets the content, which pipeline gets measured, and what the December revenue gate is actually reading.

The agency's recommendation: ship the schools campaign as the signal and authority motion, because it is built, approved in substance, and produces the market evidence the December gate needs. Name providers as the revenue motion, because they buy without committee procurement, they have a commercial reason to buy, the re-run economics are the only ones in the corpus that work for a solo founder, and a provider that adopts ETI360 carries its evidence to every school on their book. Schools are the audience. Providers are the distribution.

What would flip it: if three or more schools reply to waves 1 through 3 with an itinerary and at least one accepts a paid Simulation before 15 October, schools become the revenue motion and the provider push waits. That is a decidable test on a date that already exists in the business plan, and it is the right way to settle a question that has now been open for two months.

How the assigned lens shaped this recommendation

A B2B growth agency reads a company through its funnel, and this one has an extraordinary top and an absent middle. That is why this report spends more of its length on a missing offer page, a contact form that does not record its source, and a UTM parameter than on brand or creative: the brand is already good and the creative is already on standard, while three of the four things needed to know whether any of it works do not exist.

The lens produced four judgments a different lens might not have reached. First, the campaign platform is the concept that produces a search and answer-engine asset, not the one that produces the most beautiful posts. Second, the founder profile outranks the company page, because reach is a distribution question rather than a branding one. Third, Facebook is declined outright, because a channel that cannot reach the buyer costs production time whatever its budget. Fourth, and most consequentially, the free offer is treated as a commercial instrument with a cost and a conversion rate rather than as a generosity, which is what turns it from an obligation into a pipeline and a research asset.

The one thing the lens deliberately did not touch is the discipline that makes ETI360 credible: the boundary language, the claims restraint, the fictitious-school disclosure, and the refusal to certify anything. Those are conversion assets in this category, not constraints on it. A growth plan that traded any of them for a shorter funnel would be trading the only durable advantage the company has.
