01

Executive recommendation

Make one category choice, then make every channel prove it.

Observed ETI360 has built an unusually substantial body of visible work before it has built visible market proof. The sample trip files are specific, polished, and useful. The current brand makes that substance harder to buy by alternating among advisory firm, risk governance consultancy, intelligence provider, framework, document service, and internal platform language.

One-page strategy summary

ETI360 should own educational travel readiness.

Central problem

Prospects can see many outputs but cannot yet name the category, know which offer to buy first, or distinguish sample proof from customer proof.

Market opportunity

Occupy the space between self-assembled school paperwork, provider documents, and generic approval software: the specialist that turns trip information into one decision-ready evidence file.

Positioning

Independent educational travel readiness specialist. Keep the brand line Risk intelligence for school trips and the hero Every trip, decision-ready.

Campaign

The Decision File. Each communication shows one real artifact, names the decision moment it supports, and routes to one relevant next step.

Priority audience

International-school operational approvers: Heads, Deputy Heads, Directors of Operations, and the person accountable for the trip approval process.

Secondary audience

Trip or activities coordinators who assemble information and influence whether a new approach is adopted. Providers are a later, separate market-development track.

Website role

Evidence room and qualification layer. It must explain the category in seconds, show the sample file, and make the paid entry path clear.

Channel roles

Founder LinkedIn earns reach and trust. The company page holds the institutional record. Facebook verifies legitimacy and mirrors this campaign organically, without becoming an acquisition priority.

Conversion mechanism

Personal email or founder post → sample Decision File → trip-readiness briefing → paid 90-minute Duty Manager Simulation at the standing $3,000 pilot price → scoped Tier 2 work.

Commercial discipline

Do not give away a complete reviewed trip file. The no-cost step is a bounded briefing using one upcoming trip. The Simulation remains the sold entry product.

The leadership decision

Commit for 90 days to one position and one paid entry motion: ETI360 as the independent educational travel readiness specialist for school leaders, with the Duty Manager Simulation as the first sold engagement.

The first five actions

  1. Recommended Approve the category, formal positioning, and The Decision File campaign platform in this report. Freeze new product names for 90 days.
  2. Recommended Correct the customer-facing claims and naming sweep before outreach: remove ISO references from public surfaces, remove baseline scores and percentages, replace “advisory firm” and “advises,” and remove stale HIA and Live Ops artifacts.
  3. Recommended Revise the campaign’s no-cost offer from a complete trip file to a bounded trip-readiness briefing. Make the $3,000 Duty Manager Simulation the first priced next step.
  4. Recommended Build one conversion page around the Decision File, with one primary CTA: “Arrange a trip-readiness briefing,” and a secondary CTA: “See the sample trip file.”
  5. Recommended Verify contact delivery and conversion events, standardize UTM naming, then send the first 50 founder-reviewed emails and publish the first two social posts.

Inferred The commercial constraint is not awareness alone. It is the absence of a coherent bridge from impressive sample artifacts to a priced, low-risk engagement. The brand must now reduce choice, not add more proof.

What the market already says too often

Overused language

Safety, compliance, peace of mind, end-to-end, real-time, platform, integrated, seamless, 360-degree, best practice, duty of care, and AI-powered. These terms either overclaim, blur category, or make ETI360 sound interchangeable.

Credible territory to own

The decision-ready trip file. The same trip, prepared at the right depth for leadership, coordinators, teachers, parents, and the school’s duty manager. The proof is visible in the artifacts, not asserted in adjectives.

The strategic white space

Recommended Define the category around readiness, not risk technology or compliance. “Readiness” accommodates the annual baseline, the trip review, the Simulation, the Duty Manager Dashboard, and the post-trip record without implying certification. “Educational travel” is narrower and more ownable than travel risk. “Specialist” signals depth without violating the prohibition on advisory language.

ETI360 should not claim to be the only company with a capability. It should become the company whose category and proof are easiest to repeat: educational travel readiness, made visible in the decision file.

Creative direction

Let the artifact carry the authority.

Visual tone

Editorial, institutional, precise, and quiet. Use generous margins, firm rules, and information hierarchy. Avoid SaaS dashboards as decoration, lifestyle adventure montages, gradients, rounded-card abundance, and staged boardroom photography.

Photography

Use place and journey photography only where it helps a public-facing document feel real: parent materials, destination resources, and campaign hero moments. Governance documents use evidence-bearing imagery such as maps, routes, locations, and interface captures.

Diagram style

Simple three-band and evidence-flow diagrams with labeled arrows, not illustrations. The viewer should understand a relationship in five seconds. One diagram, one claim.

Typography

Source Serif 4 for campaign ideas, questions, and section openings. IBM Plex Sans for labels, evidence, captions, UI, tables, and CTAs. Preserve current contrast between editorial authority and operational clarity.

Color

Navy is the field, gold is the signal, parchment is the reading surface. Gold should mark hierarchy and decision points, not become body text on white. Status colors remain functional and restrained.

Layout

One primary artifact per frame. Pair it with three items only: the reader, the decision moment, and the artifact’s role. Use square corners and thin rules. Do not create collages of many unreadable pages.

Iconography

Use only when labels alone are slower: reader roles, timing, location, contact, and status. Use a consistent line weight and no illustrative icon scenes.

Data visualization

Show values with source, unit, period, and implication. Prefer route profiles, timelines, status rows, and small multiples. Never use decorative gauges or public Baseline percentages.

Motion and video

Use short screen captures only when state change is the point: a check-in arrives, a route day opens, an incident timeline advances. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Do not commission brand film in the first 90 days.

Accessibility

WCAG AA contrast, descriptive alt text, keyboard-visible focus, 44-pixel interactive targets, captions and transcripts, no color-only meaning, logical heading order, responsive tables, and printable pages.
